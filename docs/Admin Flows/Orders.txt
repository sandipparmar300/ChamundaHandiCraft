Module 06 - Order Management
1. Module Overview
Module Name

Order Management

Purpose

The Order Management module manages the complete lifecycle of customer orders from order placement through fulfillment, shipping, delivery, returns, refunds, and closure. It provides administrators with full visibility and control over order processing while ensuring accurate inventory, payment, and customer communication.

Business Goals
Centralize order processing.
Track every order status.
Streamline fulfillment workflow.
Improve customer experience.
Reduce manual errors.
Integrate payment, inventory, shipping, and invoicing.
Maintain complete audit history.

23. Business Rules
Order numbers must be unique.
Stock is reserved immediately after successful order placement (or payment, based on configuration).
Orders cannot move directly from New to Shipped; required intermediate statuses must be followed.
Delivered orders cannot be edited.
Cancelled orders cannot be shipped.
Returns are allowed only within the configured return window.
Refunds require approval based on business policy.
Invoice numbers must be unique and sequential.
Every status change is recorded in the audit log.
Inventory is automatically updated during order fulfillment, cancellation, and approved returns.

27. Acceptance Criteria
Orders progress through the defined lifecycle without invalid transitions.
Inventory, payments, invoices, and shipments remain synchronized with order status.
Shipping labels and invoices are generated accurately.
Returns and refunds follow configurable approval workflows.
Customer notifications are sent automatically for key status changes.
Search, filtering, exports, and reports work across all order states.
All significant actions are recorded in the audit log.
Role-based permissions restrict access to order operations.
The module supports future enhancements such as split shipments, partial refunds, and multi-warehouse fulfillment without requiring architectural changes.
Recommendation for Enterprise Design

Since you're building this as a .NET 8 Modular Monolithic application, I recommend treating Order as the central business entity that coordinates with:

Inventory Module (reserve/release stock)
Payment Module (payment and refund status)
Shipping Module (courier and tracking)
Customer Module (order history and notifications)
Invoice Module (tax-compliant invoicing)
Notification Module (emails, SMS, WhatsApp, push notifications)

Keeping these concerns modular while orchestrating them through the Order module will make the system easier to maintain and extend as your business grows.
